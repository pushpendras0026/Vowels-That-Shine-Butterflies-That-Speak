Experiments

Every phoneme: beyond vowels

The project started with the 12 vowels. Here we experimented further: plosives, nasals, fricatives, approximants, affricates and diphthongs were each turned into a thin-film stack, and those films were compared with butterfly and beetle films. These are the results. Different classes of sound give different colours, and we report that as a finding.

What was done, and how exact it is

  1. One real IPA-chart recording per phoneme (Wikimedia Commons). A rule for each class chooses the frames that belong to the target sound: the burst for plosives, the murmur for nasals, the frication for fricatives. Their LPC polynomials are averaged. approx
  2. That polynomial is the film. Layer peeling turns it into a stack whose reflectance is the polynomial's response, and from that we get a colour under D65. exact for the polynomial as given.
  3. Swatches here use the full-band mapping on a realizable film, in reflection (the same convention as the frame strips). On-screen brightness is exposure-normalised. artistic (display only)
  4. The "film's voice" (audio_synth) is the same polynomial spoken again with an excitation that suits its class: glottal pulses, noise, mixed, or burst. approx
  5. Each phoneme's film is compared with butterfly films by reflectance spectrum and colour. Phonemes are not compared by spectrogram. The "twin" is a stack made of real materials that has the same colour. approx
Honest caveats
  • All-pole envelope. LPC models only poles. Nasals also have zeros, from the nasal side branch. In fricatives and plosives the sound source sits inside the tract (turbulence at a constriction, or the burst at release). Every LPC polynomial with |k| < 1 still peels into a passive film, so the colour is well defined. The tube reading of it is only approximate.
  • 6 modelled phonemes. The recordings for e, ɛ, o, ʒ, tʃ and dʒ could not be downloaded, so these sounds are modelled. e, ɛ and o come from Hillenbrand men's vowel polynomials. ʒ is the ʃ polynomial with voicing, so it has the same colour as ʃ by construction. tʃ and dʒ are LAR mixes: 0.25·t + 0.75·ʃ and 0.25·d + 0.75·ʃ. They carry a modelled badge.
  • p, b, t, d use the fallback rule. The burst was detected but failed the "noise-like first 10 ms" test, so all frames were averaged, including the context vowel. Those colours partly belong to the vowel. k and g use the burst rule.
  • There is one speaker per sound, often recorded in a syllable. Diphthongs are interpolated between vowel films (LAR) and were not recorded.

Nearest real species for each phoneme

Nearest = highest correlation of the reflectance spectra over 380–780 nm at 0°. ΔE₀₀ is the colour difference. A negative r means no species has a similar spectral shape.

Fluorescence under blacklight approx QY, dye strength illustrative

Some swallowtails (for example Papilio nireus) carry a fluorescent pigment (papiliochrome) inside their photonic scales. Here every phoneme film is given that dye in every layer (core/fluorescence.py). It absorbs violet/UV and emits green around 505 nm. The film then decides how much of that light escapes upwards. Paint a word approximate spelling → sound

English spelling is not phonetic. This uses a small greedy table: digraphs such as sh, ch, tch, th, ng, ph, ck, qu, ee, oo, ou, ow, oi, ai, igh, er and ar; one letter per sound otherwise; "magic e" lengthens the vowel (make, shine); and a handful of whole words (the, you, hello…). th is always read as voiceless θ except in "the". You can also type IPA symbols directly (ʃ, ŋ, tʃ, eɪ…). A striped chip means that sound is not in the data. Playback strings together each phoneme's clip, cut to 0.45 s each, so it does not blend them into speech.

Data: web/data/sounds.json (python -m tools.build_sounds) and web/data/phonemes_experiment.json (python -m research.run_all, section 10).
